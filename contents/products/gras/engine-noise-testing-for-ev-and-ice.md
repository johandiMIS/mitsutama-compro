# Engine Noise Testing for EV and ICE

## Overview

A lot of noise originates from the engine itself, but a thorough engine sound check must
also account for the gearbox, belt drive system, pumps and cooling fans — as well as
components like the A/C compressor and power steering motors. These systems directly
contribute to a vehicle's acoustic comfort. Some sounds provide vital feedback to the
driver; other elements of engine noise testing focus on eliminating annoying or disruptive
frequencies.

Noise from the engine bay also affects external pass-by noise, which is regulated by law.

## Why It Matters for OEMs

Measurements in the engine bay give detailed information about the noise — source
location, source strength, frequency content, engine order content and time variation —
and explain the physical cause. That result guides engineers on how to reduce unwanted
noise.

Combined with interior or exterior measurements, engine bay data reveals how well acoustic
packages (sound insulation and absorption) actually perform. Measurements are made on
development test vehicles, on production follow-up vehicles, and for benchmarking, so that
customer expectations for a new vehicle can be met.

## Acoustic Test Types

| Test type | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Near-field | Standardised positions close to each source; a noise indicator for comparison between test vehicles and verification of engine calibrations |
| Far-field | Sound power estimation in a hemi-anechoic chamber with an NVH chassis dynamometer |
| Sound intensity | Noise source location; tolerant to the acoustic environment, suited to idle or fixed-rpm conditions |
| Microphone arrays | Sound source location via beamforming and acoustic holography, visualised in real time |
| Acoustic transfer function (ATF) | Average insertion loss for requirement verification, benchmarking, interior noise estimation and CAE correlation |

Far-field engineering methods need only three to four microphones; precision methods
require a minimum of 20, typically positioned in a hemisphere around the engine.

ATF testing determines results as an average of several paths — commonly up to 12 source
locations and 36 response positions, so the final number of measurements can run to
several hundred.

## Engine Noise Testing on Electric Vehicles

Since there is no low-frequency engine noise in EVs, many other noises are no longer
masked. New high-frequency tonal noise from the electric motor and the inverter is very
disturbing — especially at high speeds, where the motor starts howling. Although this
occurs at low sound pressure levels, it can be quite annoying for the driver.

On electric vehicles it is increasingly important to measure well at higher frequencies.
In an environment with more high-frequency content, extra care is needed to measure and
map content distribution reliably, making repeatable and reproducible measurement
positions more important than ever.
