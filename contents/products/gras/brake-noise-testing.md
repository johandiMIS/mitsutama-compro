# Brake Noise Testing

## Overview

Brake noise is a chassis-related noise and a significant problem for car manufacturers.
It is caused by friction-induced vibrations that make the brake system radiate noise,
causing irritation to the car owner and anyone near the vehicle. Brake noise leads to poor
customer satisfaction survey results and high warranty costs, so developing brake systems
with minimum noise issues is highly prioritised across the automotive industry.

## Noise Characteristics

For the disc brake — the most common brake type:

| Band | Character |
| --- | --- |
| 100 Hz – 1 kHz | Low frequency, often broadband: scraping, grinding, grunt, groan, moan or judder |
| 1 – 5 kHz | Medium to high-frequency tonal noise. Friction-induced, typically from coupled modes between pads and rotor causing out-of-plane vibrations. Starts at specific conditions of brake pressure, rotor temperature and environment |
| Above 5 kHz | High-frequency tonal noise — brake squeal, sometimes called squeak. Friction-induced, exciting in-plane resonances of the brake rotor |

## Development Process

Once brake performance requirements are defined, the process runs:

1. Concept selection
2. Design review and best practice checks
3. Computer-aided engineering (CAE) noise predictions
4. Component testing and modal analysis — verification of modes for all brake components,
   especially pads and rotor, plus CAE correlation
5. System test, brake squeal
6. Vehicle test, started after approval of the system test

The brake dynamometer squeal test and the wheelhouse test both involve microphones.

## Acoustic Test Types

### Brake Dynamometer Squeal Noise Test

Performed in an NVH brake dynamometer according to **SAE J2521**. Brake squeal is defined
as 1–17 kHz. Procedure:

- The complete brake system is mounted in a fixture containing a vehicle corner — one
  quarter of the full chassis with suspension
- Several thousand brake conditions are tested, varying wheel speed, brake pressure, rotor
  temperature, environmental conditions and brake wear
- A measurement microphone records the brake squeal
- Accelerometers register vibrations for correlation to the noise
- 3D laser vibrometers or acoustic cameras are sometimes used for root cause investigation

Where squeal exceeds acceptable levels, this test evaluates a range of countermeasures:
modifications to brake pads, lining material composition, pad design, added shims, mass
loading and damping, or modification of the rotor or caliper.

### Wheelhouse Test

The final road test, done at precisely defined locations and conditions over long periods
to capture all important driving, environmental and brake conditions. Noise is recorded
inside the vehicle and in the wheelhouse simultaneously with brake vibrations, brake
pressure, brake temperature, driving conditions, deceleration and subjective impressions.

The test is repeated across many vehicles and long mileages in all seasons, especially
winter. A typical OEM programme uses 25 cars, each with four microphones in the wheelhouse,
a reference microphone at the driver's ear and at least four accelerometers, driving
dedicated test cycles for 50,000 km (30,000 miles) — plus soaking in cooling facilities
and driving on splash tracks before braking.

## Brake Noise Testing on Electric Vehicles

Most EVs and HEVs are heavier than ICE vehicles, so the brake system is designed for
braking higher mass. At the same time there is increased focus on brake noise because the
auditory masking from the engine disappears.

Traditional brake systems are used less in an electric vehicle, because regeneration during
deceleration charges the battery and in many cases controls vehicle speed without using the
brake pedal. On-road acoustic brake testing is therefore required.
