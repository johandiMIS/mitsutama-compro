# Car Buzz, Squeak and Rattle Noise Testing

## Overview

Buzz, squeak and rattle (BSR) are noises produced by parts installed in such a way that
they create annoying sounds. All three are caused by relative motion between installed
parts, which makes them interact — sometimes only under certain environmental conditions.

| Noise | Cause |
| --- | --- |
| Buzz | Originates from the structure or part itself |
| Squeak | Friction between parts |
| Rattle | Impact |

## Why It Matters

BSR is a well-known source of extreme annoyance to the driver and the reason for many
customer complaints, and it forms an important part of the overall perception of a
vehicle. Even at low sound levels, BSR noise is often audible because of its intermittent
character and because it typically occupies a frequency range where sound masking is low.

A vehicle without buzz, squeak and rattle is associated with high quality, which is why
achieving it matters to OEMs.

## The Design Challenge

Masking levels are being set lower and lower, especially in electric vehicles, while at
the same time the demand for less weight increases the design problems in preventing BSR
noise.

Chassis and structural design, material selection and controlled tolerance chains are all
important elements in avoiding BSR. These must be specified in design targets and design
guidelines all the way from prototype to production, and should be constantly measured
with reliable measurement microphones.

## BSR Testing on Electric Vehicles

BSR noise is more noticeable in electric vehicles because the absence of engine noise
makes small interior sounds easier to hear. As EV cabins are quieter, controlling buzz,
squeak and rattle becomes essential to maintaining a high-quality driving experience.
