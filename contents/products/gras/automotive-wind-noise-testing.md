# Automotive Wind Noise Testing

## Overview

Aeroacoustics is the branch of acoustics that studies noise generation via turbulent fluid
motion or aerodynamic forces interacting with surfaces. It matters in the design of
vehicles, aircraft, wind turbines, buildings and bridges, where the goal is to minimise
vibration and noise.

Two noise sources are important: external noise and aerodynamic noise (pseudo-noise).

## The Core Problem

Most aeroacoustic test scenarios are expensive and time-consuming, and theoretical tests
based on calculation software are difficult to simulate realistically. Because of the
complexity of boundary layer pressure variations, simulations often require multiple
validation and correction phases.

Specifically:

- It is difficult to quantify and understand turbulence and flow-induced noise (boundary
  layer noise) on different vehicles — aircraft, cars, drones
- Simulation tools are slow and imprecise due to the stochastic behaviour of turbulence
- Experimental tests are costly because they need anechoic wind tunnels
- Traditional microphone profile heights have made experimental tests imprecise
- Mounting, demounting and remounting sensors has been tedious and not user-friendly

## Boundary Layer Measurements

The main focus of automotive wind noise testing is measurement in boundary layers. The
central interest is separating the acoustic signal from flow-induced turbulent noise.
Purposes span quantifying sound power radiated from a structure, sound source localisation
using microphone arrays, and quantifying the turbulent stresses a structure is subjected
to.

Boundary layer theory dictates that measurements must be made **exactly on the surface**
of the structure, as the statistics of the flow deviate quickly with increasing distance
from the boundary. Care must also be taken not to alter the geometry of the structure, as
that may drastically change the flow.

## Sources of Aerodynamic Noise

### Vehicle Form

A-pillars, side mirrors, windscreen wipers, antenna, underbody and glazing.

### Open Cavities

Two main sources: large cavities such as an open sunroof or window — booming or buffeting
— and smaller cavities such as door gaps, tailgate and gaps in body panels.

### Body Sealing

Unlike vehicle form and open cavities, body sealing is intended to *reduce* cabin noise.
Sealing quality has a direct effect. Noise from sealing systems tends toward higher
frequencies inside the cabin.

Above roughly 100 km/h (60 mph), aerodynamic wind noise tends to dominate overall cabin
noise — increasingly so as engine bay and powertrain noise has been refined over the
years.

## Wind Noise Testing on Electric Vehicles

Wind noise is an important noise source alongside road noise, which is the single most
important noise source for EVs and HEVs above 55 km/h (34 mph).

The *amount* of wind noise is the same in ICE vehicles and EVs. The difference is that in
EVs wind noise makes up a much larger part — around **30 %** — of the entire noise
exposure, because other noises no longer mask it. Because wind is one of the predominant
noise sources in EVs, there is a genuine need for new microphone positioning possibilities
that allow a more comprehensive understanding of noise fields and sources.

Measurements on EVs can be performed using nose cones, wall-mounted microphones or
surface-mounted microphones.

## Microphone Technologies

### UTP — Ultra-Thin Precision Microphones

Combine the precision and reliability of GRAS measurement microphones with an extremely
low profile of **less than 1 mm** and minimum turbulence influence. Designed for in-situ
boundary layer testing where neutral impact and high precision matter. All LX versions
have TEDS.

- High-precision measurement microphones
- Ideal for low-boundary-layer testing and confined spaces
- Fast mounting and remounting with consistent geometry
- Easy in-situ verification
- Wide linear frequency and dynamic range

### Surface Microphones

For in-situ boundary layer testing where non-invasive mounting is necessary, and well
suited to in-flow testing of full-scale objects in wind tunnels. Height kept at **2.5 mm**,
with a fairing that reduces self-generated turbulence. Integrated preamplifier with
plug-and-measure (TEDS).

### Flush-Mount Microphones

For fitting into very confined spaces and narrow structures. Installation height of **less
than 10 mm**, so they can be integrated into virtually any design without sacrificing
aerodynamic properties.
