# Vehicle Interior Noise on Electric Vehicles

## Overview

Vehicle interior noise, vibration and harshness (NVH) covers a number of different tests,
all related to noise inside the vehicle originating from the road, body, chassis and
vehicle components.

## Main Focus Areas

| Area | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Total interior noise | Testing the general experience of a vehicle's acoustic comfort |
| Road noise | How road excitation contributes to interior noise, both structure-borne and airborne, depending on well-defined road surfaces and tyres |
| The sound package | Affects most of the NVH areas |
| Component noise | HVAC, power windows and other systems — mostly electrical, belt-driven or hydraulic |
| Brake noise | Chassis-related noise, a significant problem for manufacturers |
| Computer model validation | Testing on prototype parts or vehicles |

## Why EVs Are Harder

Measuring inside the cabin is more challenging on an EV than an ICE vehicle:

- The general increase in high-frequency content makes TPA and ENR type measurements more
  difficult. Many sound sources have very limited energy at 20 kHz, which makes transfer
  path analysis from the engine bay harder
- Many EVs and HEVs use a more distributed powertrain with individual electric motors.
  Combined with wind, road and tyre noise — all dominant — this makes **pressure
  microphones** more relevant in the cabin than directional free-field microphones
- **Active noise cancellation:** phase is very hard to control inside the cabin, and if
  not controlled it can amplify the noise instead
- **Passive noise cancellation:** traditional approaches such as the acoustic package and
  anti-vibration mounts are still used, but need adjustment to the new environment

## Three Test Levels

### Vehicle Test

Typically two to four microphones at ear level inside the vehicle, distributed between
driver and passenger seats. Performed on test tracks, on an NVH chassis dynamometer, in
the NVH laboratory, or in a hemi-anechoic test cell.

- **Total interior noise** — on a smooth road test track while accelerating from low to
  high speed; analysis of sound pressure level (SPL) and Articulation Index (AI) versus
  speed
- **Road noise** — at dedicated NVH test tracks with smooth, coarse or grooved surfaces;
  SPL, third-octave and narrow band spectra and AI at different speeds. Extra focus on
  tyre cavity resonance noise around 200–250 Hz and tread noise
- **Component noise** — tested during typical usage conditions but isolated, in a
  hemi-anechoic test cell
- **Brake noise** — microphones at ear level and in the wheelhouse on a dedicated test
  route

### System Test — Acoustic Transfer Function

- ATF from engine bay to interior to verify the sound package; noise transfer function
  (NTF) tests to measure structural noise paths, with the vehicle in a hemi-anechoic test
  cell
- Noise source contribution from floor, dashboard and doors, measured with a microphone or
  intensity probe. Acoustic cameras can locate sources and detect leakage
- Sound absorption and sound transmission loss (STL) — absorption coefficient in a
  reverberation test cell or impedance tube; STL with the object installed in an
  anechoic/reverberation suite

### Component Test

- Component noise at system level — HVAC blower noise, electric sunroof module operating
  sound — in an anechoic or hemi-anechoic test cell
- Separate components such as small electric motors or solenoids, using SPL, sound power,
  frequency or order analysis
- Brake squeal at system level for a full vehicle corner in a dedicated brake squeal
  dynamometer

### Computer Model Validation

Modal analysis and transfer function measurements, NTFs and ATFs, using impact hammers,
shakers and volume velocity sources for excitation. Mule vehicles — existing cars modified
with new concepts — are also used.
