# DAC, Power Amplifier and DSP Test and Equipment

## Overview

Audio amplifiers are essential in audio systems, designed to increase weak audio signals
to drive speakers and produce sound. While every type of amplifier boosts audio, the way
each handles signals, manages heat and affects audio quality varies significantly —
leading to different amplifier classes with distinct advantages and trade-offs.

The main amplifier types — Class A, B, AB and D — differ in sound fidelity, efficiency and
practicality, which affects which design suits which application. But the general
performance questions stay the same: how well does the device filter for noise? What is
the frequency response? Is the gain linear?

Evaluating these metrics requires rigorous testing, and that is where nuanced measurement
tactics and tools come into play.

## Measurement Challenges

Power amplifiers present a set of testing challenges not usually encountered with digital
or low-level analogue circuits, because they must supply significant **current** as well
as voltage to their loads. Factors that must be considered to produce meaningful results:

- Power supply current and voltage limitations
- Thermal characteristics of the output devices and associated heatsinks
- Resistive and reactive components of the load

Switch-mode (Class D) audio power amplifiers became popular for their smaller size, lower
weight and improved efficiency, and are progressively displacing traditional linear designs
in mainstream applications where high-quality audio matters. They require special
conditioning of the amplifier output to address out-of-band noise unique to these amps.
